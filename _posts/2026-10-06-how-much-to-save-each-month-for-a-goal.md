**The short answer:** subtract what you've already saved from your goal, then divide by the number of months until your deadline. That's how much to save each month.

> **Monthly saving = (goal − already saved) ÷ months left**

Interest on a savings account will help a little, but for goals under a few years away it's usually small, so this simple formula is a sensible plan. Any interest you earn just gets you there slightly early.

## Examples

**Holiday:** $1,200 goal, $0 saved, 10 months away.
$1,200 ÷ 10 = **$120 a month**.

**Car down payment:** $5,000 goal, $1,400 saved, 18 months away.
($5,000 − $1,400) ÷ 18 = **$200 a month**.

**Emergency fund:** $7,500 goal, $500 saved, 24 months away.
($7,500 − $500) ÷ 24 = **about $292 a month**.

Not sure what your emergency fund target should be? See [how much should your emergency fund be](/how-much-should-your-emergency-fund-be/).

## Saving for several goals at once

Calculate each goal separately, then add them up:

| Goal | Still needed | Months left | Per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holiday | $1,200 | 10 | $120 |
| Car down payment | $3,600 | 18 | $200 |
| Emergency fund | $7,000 | 24 | $292 |
| **Total** | | | **$612** |

If $612 fits in your [monthly budget](/how-to-make-a-monthly-budget/), great. Set up an automatic transfer for the total the day after payday.

## When the monthly number is too high

You have three levers:

1. **Move the date.** Giving the car down payment 30 months instead of 18 drops it from $200 to $120 a month.
2. **Lower the goal.** A $900 holiday instead of $1,200 saves $30 a month.
3. **Prioritize.** Fund the most important goal fully first, then add the next. Most people put a starter emergency fund first.

## Recalculate as you go

Life changes the numbers. Recalculate monthly, using what you've actually saved and the months that are actually left. If you got ahead, your monthly amount falls. If you skipped a month, it rises a little, which is better than discovering a big shortfall at the deadline.

## Where to keep goal savings

For goals within a few years, a high-yield savings account at an FDIC-insured bank keeps the money safe and earning some interest. Stocks are generally better suited to goals five or more years away, because their value can drop sharply in the short term.

For smaller, predictable costs that repeat every year (gifts, car registration), the same formula applies, and they're usually called [sinking funds](/sinking-funds-explained/).

## Quick checklist

- Write each goal, its target amount and its date.
- Note what you've already saved toward each.
- Divide what's still needed by the months left.
- Add up the monthly amounts and check they fit your budget.
- Automate the transfer.
- Recalculate once a month.

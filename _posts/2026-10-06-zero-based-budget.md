**The short answer:** in a zero-based budget, you assign every dollar of your monthly income to a category (bills, spending, savings or debt) until **income minus assigned money equals zero**. It doesn't mean you spend everything. Savings and extra debt payments count as jobs too.

## Why it works

Money without a plan tends to leak. A coffee here, a delivery fee there. When every dollar is assigned before the month starts, unplanned spending has to come from somewhere specific, which makes trade-offs visible: "if I buy this, it comes out of groceries."

## How to set up a zero-based budget

1. **Write down this month's take-home income.** Only money you actually have or will definitely receive.
2. **Assign money to fixed bills** first: housing, utilities, insurance, minimum payments.
3. **Assign money to essentials** that vary: groceries, fuel, medication.
4. **Fund your [sinking funds](/sinking-funds-explained/)** for irregular costs like car repairs and gifts.
5. **Assign money to goals:** emergency fund, extra debt payments, savings.
6. **Assign what's left to wants:** eating out, entertainment, hobbies.
7. **Check the total.** Income minus all assignments should equal exactly $0. If it's positive, assign the rest. If it's negative, cut until it balances.

## Worked example: $3,600 take-home

| Category | Assigned |
|---|---|
| Rent | $1,250 |
| Utilities and phone | $230 |
| Car insurance and fuel | $290 |
| Groceries | $450 |
| Credit card minimum | $90 |
| Sinking funds | $200 |
| Emergency fund | $250 |
| Extra credit card payment | $300 |
| Retirement (IRA) | $150 |
| Eating out | $140 |
| Entertainment and hobbies | $100 |
| Personal and household | $100 |
| Miscellaneous buffer | $50 |
| **Total assigned** | **$3,600** |
| **Income minus assigned** | **$0** |

## What happens when you overspend?

You will, especially in the first few months. When a category runs out, **move money from another category** rather than borrowing from next month or using a card. Overspent groceries by $40? Take it from eating out or entertainment. The budget still balances to zero, and you learn which estimates need adjusting.

## Zero-based budgeting with irregular income

If your pay varies, two approaches help:

- **Budget your lowest typical month.** Assign extra income to goals when it arrives.
- **Live on last month's income.** Build a one-month buffer in savings, then each month budget only the money you earned the month before. It takes time to build, but it removes most of the stress of uneven paychecks.

## Zero-based vs 50/30/20

| | Zero-based | [50/30/20](/50-30-20-rule-explained/) |
|---|---|---|
| Detail | Every category, every dollar | Three broad buckets |
| Effort | 30 to 60 minutes a month, plus check-ins | 10 minutes |
| Best for | Getting control, paying off debt, tight budgets | A quick health check |

Many people start with 50/30/20 to see the big picture, then move to zero-based when they want more control.

## Tips

- **Budget before the month starts**, or right after payday.
- **Include a small buffer line.** You'll forget something.
- **Check in weekly.** Five minutes comparing planned vs actual is enough.
- **Don't aim for perfect.** If you're within a few percent by month three, it's working.

**The short answer:** add up what you actually take home, subtract your fixed bills, set a spending limit for each flexible category, decide what the leftover goes toward (savings or debt), and then track what really happens for a month. Adjust, and repeat.

A budget isn't a punishment. It's a plan for where your money goes, made before the month starts instead of discovered after it ends.

## Step 1: Use your take-home pay

Use the amount that actually lands in your bank account after tax, retirement contributions and benefits, not your salary. If your income varies (freelance, tips, shift work), use your **lowest typical month** from the last six to twelve months. Anything above that is a bonus you can assign when it arrives.

## Step 2: List your fixed costs

These are bills that are the same, or close to it, every month:

- Rent or mortgage
- Car payment and insurance
- Phone and internet
- Minimum debt payments
- Subscriptions
- Childcare

Grab last month's bank statement so you don't forget any.

## Step 3: Estimate your flexible spending

These change month to month:

- Groceries
- Eating out and takeout
- Fuel and transport
- Utilities (often seasonal)
- Personal care, clothing, entertainment

If you don't know what you spend, look at the last two or three months of statements and take the average. Most people underestimate food and small purchases, so be honest here.

## Step 4: Don't forget irregular costs

Car registration, annual subscriptions, holidays, birthdays and back-to-school costs don't come every month, but they come every year. Add up the yearly total, divide by 12, and set that aside monthly. This is called a [sinking fund](/sinking-funds-explained/), and it's the single biggest fix for budgets that "keep breaking."

## Step 5: Give the leftover a job

Income minus fixed, flexible and irregular costs is what's left. Decide now where it goes:

1. A starter emergency fund, if you don't have one. See [how much to keep](/how-much-should-your-emergency-fund-be/).
2. Extra payments on high-interest debt.
3. Savings goals: a car, a move, a holiday.
4. Retirement, beyond any workplace contribution.

If the number is negative, you're spending more than you earn. Go back to the flexible categories first, since they're the easiest to change quickly.

## Step 6: Track it for one month, then adjust

Your first budget will be wrong, and that's normal. Once a week, compare what you planned to what you actually spent in each category. At the end of the month, move money between categories based on what you learned. Most people have a budget that works by month three.

## Example: a $4,000 monthly take-home budget

| Category | Planned |
|---|---|
| Rent | $1,400 |
| Utilities | $180 |
| Phone and internet | $110 |
| Car payment | $320 |
| Car insurance | $140 |
| Fuel | $160 |
| Groceries | $500 |
| Eating out | $150 |
| Subscriptions | $40 |
| Personal and household | $120 |
| Irregular costs (sinking funds) | $150 |
| Credit card minimums | $105 |
| **Total spending** | **$3,375** |
| **Left to assign** | **$625** |

That $625 might be split as $300 extra on a credit card, $200 to an emergency fund and $125 to a holiday fund. Every dollar has a job, so nothing quietly disappears.

## Which budgeting method should you use?

The steps above work with any method. A few popular frameworks:

- **[The 50/30/20 rule](/50-30-20-rule-explained/):** a quick starting split of needs, wants and savings.
- **[Zero-based budgeting](/zero-based-budget/):** give every single dollar a job until income minus spending equals zero.
- **Envelope or cash budgeting:** spend only what's in each category's "envelope."

Pick whichever feels least like a chore. A simple budget you check every week beats a perfect one you abandon.

## Tips that make it stick

- **Schedule a 15-minute weekly check-in.** Same day, same time.
- **Automate savings and debt payments** for the day after payday.
- **Round up your estimates** a little. Coming in under budget feels better than constantly going over.
- **Leave a small "miscellaneous" line** for the things you'll forget.

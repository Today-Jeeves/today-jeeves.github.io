**The short answer:** the 50/30/20 rule splits your **after-tax** income into three buckets: **50% for needs**, **30% for wants** and **20% for savings and extra debt payments**. It's a quick starting point, not a strict law. If your rent alone is 45% of your pay, adjust the split rather than give up.

The rule was popularized by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi in their book *All Your Worth*.

## What goes in each bucket

**Needs (50%)** are things you must pay to live and work:

- Rent or mortgage
- Utilities
- Groceries (basic, not takeout)
- Insurance
- Transport to work
- Minimum debt payments
- Childcare

**Wants (30%)** are things that make life better but you could cut:

- Eating out and takeout
- Streaming and subscriptions
- Hobbies and entertainment
- Holidays
- Upgrades (a nicer phone plan, a bigger car than you need)

**Savings and debt (20%)**:

- Emergency fund
- Retirement contributions beyond what's already taken from your paycheck
- Savings goals
- Payments **above** the minimum on debt

## Examples at three income levels

| Monthly take-home | Needs (50%) | Wants (30%) | Savings/debt (20%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $3,000 | $1,500 | $900 | $600 |
| $4,500 | $2,250 | $1,350 | $900 |
| $6,000 | $3,000 | $1,800 | $1,200 |

At $4,500 a month, that's $900 a month toward savings and debt, or $10,800 a year.

## How to use it

1. Find your monthly take-home pay.
2. Add up your current needs, wants and savings from the last month or two of statements.
3. Compare each to its target percentage.
4. Adjust the bucket that's furthest off.

The most common finding is that wants are over 30% because they hide in small purchases, and savings are under 20%.

## When to bend the rule

**High-cost housing.** In many cities, rent alone is 40% or more of take-home pay. A 60/20/20 or 60/30/10 split may be more realistic. Protect the savings bucket first and squeeze wants.

**Paying off high-interest debt.** Some people temporarily go 50/20/30, with 30% to savings and debt, to get out of debt faster. See [snowball vs avalanche](/debt-snowball-vs-avalanche/) for how to aim that money.

**Lower incomes.** If needs take 70% or more, the rule can feel impossible. Start with any savings amount, even 5%, and build a [starter emergency fund](/how-much-should-your-emergency-fund-be/) first.

**Higher incomes.** If needs are only 30%, the rule leaves a lot of room for wants. Consider pushing savings well above 20%.

## 50/30/20 vs other budgets

- It's **simpler** than [zero-based budgeting](/zero-based-budget/), which assigns every dollar to a specific category.
- It doesn't tell you exactly what to spend on groceries or fuel. For that, build a [category-level monthly budget](/how-to-make-a-monthly-budget/) and use 50/30/20 as a sanity check on the totals.

## The bottom line

Use 50/30/20 to see whether your spending is roughly balanced. If it isn't, it tells you which bucket to fix. Then build a detailed budget so you know where each dollar is going.

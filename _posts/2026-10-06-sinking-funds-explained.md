**The short answer:** a sinking fund is money you set aside a little at a time for a **known** future expense. Add up what a cost will be, divide by the months until it's due, and save that amount each month. When the bill arrives, the money is already there.

## Why budgets "break"

Most budgets cover monthly bills well. What blows them up are the costs that come once or twice a year: car insurance paid annually, a new set of tires, holiday gifts, an annual subscription. They feel like emergencies, but they aren't surprises. They're predictable expenses that weren't planned for.

## Sinking fund vs emergency fund

| | Sinking fund | [Emergency fund](/how-much-should-your-emergency-fund-be/) |
|---|---|---|
| For | Known, planned costs | Unexpected, urgent costs |
| Example | Holiday gifts in December | Sudden job loss |
| Amount | Set by the expense | 3 to 6 months of essentials |
| Used | On schedule | Only when something goes wrong |

Keeping them separate protects your emergency fund from being drained by things that were always going to happen.

## Common sinking fund categories

- Car: insurance (if paid annually), registration, maintenance, tires
- Home: repairs, appliance replacement, renters or home insurance
- Gifts: birthdays, holidays, weddings
- Travel and holidays
- Medical: deductibles, dental, glasses
- Kids: back-to-school, activities, camps
- Annual subscriptions and memberships
- Pet costs: vet visits, grooming
- Electronics: replacing a phone or laptop

## How to calculate your monthly amount

For each fund: **target amount ÷ months until you need it = monthly saving.**

### Example

| Fund | Target | Due in | Per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holiday gifts | $600 | 6 months | $100 |
| Car registration | $180 | 9 months | $20 |
| Car maintenance | $720 | 12 months | $60 |
| Summer trip | $1,500 | 10 months | $150 |
| Annual subscriptions | $240 | 12 months | $20 |
| **Total** | | | **$350** |

That $350 a month becomes one line in your [monthly budget](/how-to-make-a-monthly-budget/). It looks like a lot until you remember you were going to spend $3,240 on these things anyway, just all at once and probably on a credit card.

If you're starting late (gifts due in two months, not six), the monthly amount will be higher this year. Next year, start the fund right after the expense so it's spread over 12 months.

## Where to keep sinking funds

You don't need a separate bank account for each one. Options:

- **One savings account, tracked on paper or in a spreadsheet.** Simple and works for most people.
- **A bank that lets you create named "buckets"** or sub-accounts inside one savings account.
- **Several savings accounts** if you like seeing each balance separately.

Whatever you choose, keep it apart from everyday checking.

## Getting started this month

1. List every non-monthly expense from the last 12 months. Your bank statements will remind you.
2. Estimate the cost and the date of each one.
3. Calculate the monthly amount for each.
4. Start with the two or three that are coming up soonest if the total feels too big.
5. Automate one transfer on payday for the total.

For bigger or longer-term targets, see [how much to save each month for a goal](/how-much-to-save-each-month-for-a-goal/).
